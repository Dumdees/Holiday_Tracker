## Monteith Holiday Manager 1.5.0

**Care Empire has been rebuilt.** The tea-break game is now a proper little strategy game about running a home-care service, and it looks the part.

- **Two sides to balance.** The work you have taken on and the team who deliver it both have to grow. A strip under the street tells you which one a minute of takings is better spent on, in a sentence. Nothing in the game can ever cost you income: every purchase is worth having, and the shop says exactly how much and how long it takes to pay for itself.
- **Upgrades worth thinking about.** They do different jobs: key safes that make every carer quicker, a rota app in everyone's hand, bonuses that grow the more of the work your team can cover, cheaper prices, automation, and boosters that make every tenth purchase worth more. Each tile carries one figure on its face – "+40%", "−20% to buy", "×3 a tap" – so two can be compared at a glance, and it says why you would buy that one first, what it does, and what you will see change on the street.
- **Every stage brings something new.** A stage's own shelf is drawn fresh each time from a much bigger range, so eight of the twelve things a new patch brings are ones the last patch did not have – including bonuses that bring the milestones round sooner, that carry a lopsided round, and that price everything as though you owned fewer of it.
- **Three big decisions.** Who you work for, how you grow, and what you are known for. Each is a different shape rather than a different number, so which is best depends on how you play and how long you stay on a patch. They stay with you now and are opened up again every third hand-over, rather than being asked every couple of minutes.
- **Handing over is a real decision.** The card tells you what this run has to earn and – once it is measured how fast you are actually growing – roughly how long that will take. You can hand over the moment you have done it, or stay on the patch a while: staying is worth more Legacy Stars and a permanent lift to everything you will ever earn, but the stages come slower. There is a right moment and it is somewhere in the middle.
- **Two ways to run it.** Keep the team ahead of the work and nobody is rushed; keep the work ahead of the team and every round is full. Both pay, and you cannot have both at once, so where you sit between the two sides is the decision the game turns on.
- **Your own visits always count.** Tapping the street finds the nearest door that is ready, the shop puts the knocking upgrades where you can find them, and the street says what share of your money comes from visits you did yourself.
- **Coming back to it.** While the game is closed the team keeps the round going, and if they get past the finish line they hand the patch over without you – so a night away is worth several stages and the stars that go with them, and the welcome back says so.
- **Everything you buy shows up.** Key safes on doors, care folders on doorsteps, sensor lights, sat navs and liveried cars and an electric fleet, coordinators and supervisors and nurses walking the patch, every branch and academy on the skyline, and an office that wears its sign, its rating, its certificate, its noticeboard and its bunting. The biggest buys change the street itself – a pay board over the office, lights the length of the road, rosettes along the pavement, window boxes on every door.
- **Real home care.** Care packages, direct payments, council contracts, hospital discharge, framework places, NHS-funded care, nurse-led teams, the Care Certificate, guaranteed hours, and turning down calls too short to do properly. Your rating goes up when you invest in the things a good service invests in, and a reputation once earned is never lost.
- **On a phone.** The collect button comes first while the payments are collected by hand, a tap on an upgrade says what it does before you buy it, and one message shows at a time instead of covering the shop.
- **Nobody loses progress.** Older saved games are brought across onto the new street.

**Also in this release: shift patterns.** Some carers work every other weekend, or a rota that repeats every three or four weeks. When adding or editing a carer, choose *Repeats every 2 weeks* (or 3 or 4), tick the days for each week and say which week the current week is. There is a one-click fill for the usual alternate-weekends pattern. Holidays then only use up days the carer would really have worked, clash warnings only count someone on the days they would have been in, the carer's profile greys out their days off on the year calendar, and spreadsheet exports and imports carry the pattern.
